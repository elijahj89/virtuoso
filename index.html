The Premise

Talent is common.
Devotion is not.

Most children who study an instrument are taught by someone for whom music became a job. Competent, credentialed, and long since past the fire that made them pick up the instrument in the first place.

There is another group entirely: the students currently in the practice rooms at Juilliard, Curtis, and Eastman. They are the best players of their generation, they are in the most demanding years of their own study, and they are still in love with it. That intensity is contagious in a way no curriculum can manufacture.

Virtuoso exists to put that person in a room with your child — and to make it worth their while to be there.

How It Works

Four steps from enquiry to first lesson.

  1. Tell us about your musician

    Instrument, age, how far along they are, and what you're hoping for — whether that's a genuine love of playing or a conservatory audition in six years. The form below takes about three minutes.

  2. A conversation, not a questionnaire

    We call you. Matching a child to a teacher is a judgement about temperament as much as technique, and it isn't something an algorithm should be making. We want to know how your child responds to being pushed, and what a good week of practice actually looks like in your house.

  3. Meet your match

    We hand-select one or two instructors from our network and arrange a complimentary evaluation lesson with each. You watch. If the chemistry isn't right, we say so first and go back to the network — at no cost to you.

  4. Begin, and keep going

    Weekly private lessons, online or in person where geography allows. We stay involved: progress reviews each term, a standing line to us if something isn't working, and continuity planning well before an instructor graduates so no student is ever left mid-repertoire.

About Virtuoso

An academy without a building.

Virtuoso is not a school in the conventional sense. We have no campus, no faculty lounge, and no intention of building either. What we have is a carefully kept network of the strongest collegiate and graduate performers in the United States, and a serious, unhurried process for placing a child with exactly the right one.

Every instructor is currently enrolled in — or has recently completed — a degree at a leading conservatory or school of music. Every one is auditioned by us, interviewed on how they teach rather than how they play, and background-checked before they meet a single student.

We keep the network small on purpose. We would rather tell a family we don't yet have the right teacher for them than hand them a merely adequate one.

Our Values

What we will not compromise.

Four commitments that decide every instructor we accept and every match we make.

I

Excellence, without exception

We accept a small fraction of the instructors who apply. A conservatory place is the beginning of our standard, not the end of it — we audition every applicant and interview them on pedagogy before they teach for us.

II

Devotion over credentials

A résumé tells us who can play. We are looking for the ones who cannot stop talking about why a phrase works — because that is the thing a young student actually catches.

III

The student before the syllabus

A nine-year-old who wants to play film scores and a fifteen-year-old preparing an audition are not the same commission. We match to the child in front of us, and we are honest when the fit is wrong.

IV

Reciprocity

The art form that gives your child a teacher should be given something back. We pay our instructors properly, on time, and at a rate that meaningfully offsets the cost of their own training.

Giving Back

Every lesson funds two educations.

Conservatory training is among the most expensive education in America, and it leads to one of its most poorly compensated professions. The gifted young players who could teach your child brilliantly are, at this very moment, deciding whether they can afford to finish their degrees at all.

When you engage a Virtuoso instructor, your tuition goes directly to a working musician at the precise point in their life where it changes the outcome. You are not only buying your child the best teaching available — you are helping keep that teacher in the art form your child is trying to master.

Questions

Before you enquire.

If your question isn't answered here, ask it in the form below — we answer every enquiry personally.

How much do lessons cost?

Rates depend on the instructor's level of study, the lesson length, and whether lessons are online or in person. We quote a firm price after the initial conversation, before the evaluation lesson, and there is never a placement or matching fee. The evaluation lesson itself is complimentary.

Which instruments do you cover?

Piano, strings, woodwinds, brass, classical and jazz voice, guitar, and percussion, along with music theory and composition. If we don't currently have the right instructor for your instrument, we will tell you plainly rather than offer you a near-match.

What ages do you teach?

We principally serve students between roughly six and eighteen, from complete beginners through pre-conservatory candidates preparing auditions. Serious adult beginners are considered case by case.

Are lessons online or in person?

Both. Online is our default, because it lets us match your child to the right teacher rather than the nearest one — a student in Ohio can study with a violinist at Curtis. In-person instruction is available in select markets where our instructor network and your location overlap. Tell us your city in the form and we'll say honestly which is realistic for you.

How are instructors vetted?

Every applicant submits a performance audition, sits a pedagogy interview about how they would approach a real student, provides references from their own faculty, and clears a background check. We accept a small minority of those who apply.

What if the match isn't right?

Tell us, and we rematch at no charge. That is the entire point of the evaluation lesson — and frankly, we would often rather raise it first ourselves. A polite mismatch that limps along for a year is the most expensive thing that can happen to a young musician.

What happens when my child's instructor graduates?

We plan for it well in advance. Many instructors continue with us into graduate study or their performing careers. Where they don't, we arrange a deliberate handover — the outgoing instructor briefs the incoming one on repertoire, technique, and temperament, so nothing restarts from zero.

How does the money reach the instructor?

The substantial majority of what you pay goes directly to your child's teacher. Virtuoso retains a modest share to cover auditioning, matching, background checks, scheduling, and ongoing oversight. We are glad to show you the split.
